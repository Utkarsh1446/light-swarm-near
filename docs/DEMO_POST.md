## Text

Can a learner invent a grid rule without first memorising the internet? Giant chat models are the wrong tool for that question: they are expensive, opaque, and already full of the web.

Light agents are the opposite bet, and this one was built at NEAR HACKATHON. Five learners, 326,800 parameters each, same random start, each trained from scratch on its own curriculum. No LLM, no pretrained weights. They bid as solvers on an intent: a verified grid, paid when the hash matches. The NEAR testnet contract holds the bounty and will not pay a wrong grid. A dry NEAR Intents quote shows what paying a helper in USDC on Ethereum would look like. No mainnet funds move. A key for an agent can bid and reveal, and cannot cancel the task.

On protocols frozen before scoring, a learned prior beat an untrained net and a shuffled-label twin. Upbringing specialized the five. A curiosity bonus and the society-versus-best-member contrast did not clear. Those nulls are in the paper. No ARC-AGI score is claimed. Earlier work on this question saw a synthetic generalisation gate pass, a real-task confirmation fail, and a deeper-search gate fail its control.

What would convince you a small learner had actually grown a new concept, rather than searched a catalogue we wrote ourselves?
